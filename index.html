02
Computer Vision · Robotics

Vision-Based Virtual Maze Navigator

An autonomous e-puck robot that navigates an unknown 3D maze and reaches a target in Webots from camera pixels only — no odometry, no map, no distance sensors in the control loop. Three perception streams (Canny edge density across three vertical zones, right-camera near-field brightness, HSV target detection) feed a reactive policy that prioritises obstacle avoidance over target seeking. Key finding: walls register as edge density collapsing to zero, not spiking — which is why the edge-based detectors survived blur and dimming while the one absolute-threshold detector broke. Every frame's detector flags and wheel commands are logged to CSV for the robustness experiments.

// 07 — research

Research & publications

Under review · NeurIPS 2026 arXiv:2605.12898

When Do LLMs Generate Realistic Social Networks? A Multi-Dimensional Study of Culture, Language, Scale, and Method

S. H. Kilaru, S. T. Manikyala, R. Upadhyay, S. S. K. Ramavath, S. Nunavathu, D. Alharthi · May 2026

Building on homophily and structural balance theory, we formalize four LLM-based tie-formation mechanisms — sequential, global, local, and iterative — as distinct conditional distributions over edge sets. Using a fixed roster of 50 demographically grounded personas, we generate 192 verified directed networks across four cultural contexts, four prompt languages, three GPT-4.1 variants, and four prompting architectures.

  • Cultural framing measurably shifts inbreeding homophily and largest-component connectivity.
  • Political affiliation dominates tie formation under three of four methods; the global method substitutes age — prompt architecture behaves as a substantive sociological variable.
  • Model scale produces a stable divergence ranking (GPT-4.1 ↔ mini = 0.074, ↔ nano = 0.119), with the smallest variant differing qualitatively rather than just noisily.
GPT-4.1 NetworkX pandas Experimental Design
